THE MANIFESTO

Build what's
next.

Frontier Cascadia is not a classroom exercise. It is not a science fair. It is one day to build something real. Software that solves a problem, tells a story, or changes how someone sees the world. The best thing you could get out of a weekend.

We bring together the most technically ambitious high school students across the United States, put them in a room with the resources they need, and ask them to ship solutions in 12 hours.

Then we put them in front of judges who take them seriously. No participation trophies. No grade inflation. The standard is whether it works.

WHO'S BEHIND THIS

Built by students,
for students.

The story behind Frontier Cascadia is pretty simple. Many high school students across the United States are genuinely good at building things. They have ideas. They're technical. What they don't have is the space, the time, the tools, or the chance to sit in a room with people who've actually shipped products and learn from them. Most of the time, the gap between a good idea and a real project is just a runway.

Frontier Cascadia is how we're closing that gap. It's a one-day hackathon on September 12 at the Foster School of Business at the University of Washington in Seattle. All high schoolers across the United States are welcome to join us for 12 hours of building real software from scratch. Entry is free, and the prize pool offers up to $58,000 in cash and winner benefits: everything from a grand prize to free credits to company- and track-specific awards and themes. Projects get judged by engineers and professionals from Seattle's tech community on a real rubric.

Frontier Cascadia is a 501(c)(3) public charity based in Washington.

LOGISTICS
FAQ

Common questions

Who can participate?

High school students in grades 9 through 12 across the United States are welcome. Participants must be based in the United States to be eligible for prizes. You don't need to know how to code yet, but you should be ready to learn fast.

Where is it?

The Foster School of Business at the University of Washington in Seattle. Founders Hall, 4215 E Stevens Way NE. It's a short walk from the U District light rail station, and there's parking on campus.

I have the SAT that morning.

September 12 is an SAT date, so late check-in stays open until 3 PM. Flag it when you register, come straight from the test, and you'll still have hours to build. Standard prize eligibility requirements apply.

Can I start before the event?

You can plan, sketch, read docs, and set up an empty repo with boilerplate and dependencies installed. What you can't do is show up with the project already working. The logic gets written on the day, and that's the same rule for everyone.

Do I need a team?

No. You can come solo and find teammates at the event, bring your friends, or hack alone. Teams can be up to 4 people.

What should I bring?

Your laptop, a charger, and any hardware you want to use. We provide food, drinks, wifi, and power strips.

Is it really free?

Yes. Completely free. Meals, snacks, swag, cloud credits, all of it. We want cost to be zero barrier to showing up.

What can I build?

Anything that runs. Web apps, mobile apps, hardware projects, games, AI tools, browser extensions. If it works, it counts.

Do I need experience?

Some coding experience helps, but we have seen beginners build incredible things with industry experts available to answer questions and sheer determination. Don't count yourself out.
