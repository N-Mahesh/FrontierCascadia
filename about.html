Frontier Cascadia is a one-day hackathon for high school students in the Pacific Northwest. Twelve hours, real software, judged by people who ship for a living. This is who runs it and why it exists.

501(c)(3) public charity Seattle, Washington

Why this exists

The story is pretty simple. Many high school students in the Pacific Northwest are genuinely good at building things. They have ideas. They're technical. What they don't have is the space, the time, the tools, or the chance to sit in a room with people who've actually shipped products and learn from them. Most of the time, the gap between a good idea and a real project is just a runway.

Frontier Cascadia is how we're closing that gap. On September 12, 2026, 200+ high school students from across the PNW get 12 hours at the Foster School of Business at the University of Washington to build real software from scratch. Entry is free. Prizes run $10k+, from a grand prize to free credits to company and track specific awards. Projects get judged by engineers and professionals from Seattle's tech community on a real rubric.

No participation trophies. No grade inflation. The standard is whether it works.
